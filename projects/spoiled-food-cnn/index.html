Visual Food Freshness Classification with CNN

Overview

Purpose and scope

A 15.76-million-parameter TensorFlow CNN trained on 30,357 produce images to distinguish visually fresh examples from rotten ones across multiple fruit and vegetable categories.

Engineering work

What the work covered

  • Consolidated produce-specific directories into fresh and rotten labels, yielding 23,619 training images and 6,738 held-out test images.
  • Built a TensorFlow/Keras network for 256 × 256 RGB input with two convolution and max-pooling stages, a 64-unit dense layer, and sigmoid binary output.
  • Configured Adam at a 0.001 learning rate with binary cross-entropy and a 15-epoch training plan, then saved the trained network in Keras format.
  • Implemented both single-image inference and a 4 × 4 predicted-versus-actual review for inspecting model behavior across different produce types.
  • The preserved 16-image panel contains 15 correct classifications and one rotten example predicted as fresh. The notebook records approximately 98% test accuracy, although its exported training log is not present.
  • Published the original notebook alongside portable training and inference scripts, reproducibility steps, methodology, and explicit evaluation limits.
